# Prior Authorization Policy

Therapeutic exercise (CPT 97110) and knee arthroscopy with meniscectomy (CPT 29881) are the two procedures most frequently requiring prior authorization across payers in this book of business. Office visits, electrocardiograms, and basic lab panels generally do not require prior authorization.

Aetna requires prior authorization for therapeutic exercise (97110) beyond the first two visits in a plan year; failing to obtain it results in denial code CO-197. Providers should submit the authorization request at least 5 business days before the third visit to avoid a gap in coverage.

If a service is rendered without the required authorization, the payer may grant a retroactive authorization only if the request is submitted within 30 days of the date of service and includes clinical documentation showing the service was medically necessary at the time it was rendered. A retroactive authorization, once granted, is the basis for a CO-197 appeal.

Medicare does not require prior authorization for knee arthroscopy in most cases, but does require supporting documentation of a medical necessity - typically imaging results and a trial of conservative treatment - to avoid a CO-50 denial.
