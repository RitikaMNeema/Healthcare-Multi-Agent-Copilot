# HIPAA Privacy Policy

Access to protected health information follows the HIPAA minimum necessary standard: staff should be given access to only the PHI needed to perform their specific role, and no more. Viewers may see redacted, aggregated claims metrics only. Operators may look up individual claims and denial details for casework, and every such lookup is written to the audit log. Admins may additionally generate cross-payer remediation plans that reference aggregate denial patterns.

A suspected breach of unsecured PHI affecting 500 or more individuals must be reported to the Department of Health and Human Services and to affected individuals within 60 days of discovery, per the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule. Breaches affecting fewer than 500 individuals may be reported in an annual log but must still notify affected individuals within 60 days.

Patients have the right to access their own medical and billing records within 30 days of a written request.

Any export of raw, patient-identifiable claims data - as opposed to an individual lookup for casework - requires a documented business justification and notification to the privacy officer within 24 hours of the export.

Any system output that includes a patient's full identifying information (patient ID combined with procedure and payer details) alongside billing amounts should be treated as PHI-adjacent and reviewed before release outside the care team, even when the underlying use case is legitimate.
