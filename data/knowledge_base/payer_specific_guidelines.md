# Payer-Specific Guidelines

Aetna's denials concentrate heavily on prior authorization for therapeutic exercise (CPT 97110); this is the single largest driver of Aetna's overall denial rate in this book of business and should be the first target for a remediation plan aimed at Aetna.

UnitedHealthcare's claim submission portal has a shorter effective timely filing window than its stated 90 days once clearinghouse transmission delays are accounted for, which has made timely-filing denials (CO-29) UnitedHealthcare's most common denial reason. Providers submitting to UnitedHealthcare should transmit claims within 60 days of the date of service as an internal buffer.

BlueCross BlueShield has the lowest denial rate in this book of business, attributed to streamlined electronic eligibility verification at the time of scheduling, which catches most administrative and eligibility issues before the claim is ever submitted.

Medicare's denials for orthopedic procedures such as knee arthroscopy (CPT 29881) are concentrated in medical-necessity documentation gaps (CO-50); ensuring imaging results and conservative-treatment history are attached at submission time substantially reduces this category.
